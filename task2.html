Task 2 · Design a feature from customer feedback

The report should come to you

Three customers, three roles, one complaint said three ways: Zanko's reporting is pull — log in, rebuild, export, trim, forward — while their week runs on push. The feature is Scheduled Reports: subscribe the right people to a report Zanko already generates, in the version each audience needs, delivered by email on a cadence.

TL;DR — 30 seconds

  • The shared need: the information already exists in Zanko — the missing product is distribution. Every quote describes manual redistribution of a report the platform already makes.
  • The feature: Scheduled Reports — a Schedules tab in Reporting: pick an existing report, set a cadence, choose recipients with a version per audience (Full or Executive), optionally review before external sends, done. It runs until paused.
  • Deliberately out of v1: report authoring/custom builder, Slack/Teams, per-recipient custom filters, multi-report digests. Distribution is the gap; everything else dilutes it.
  • Proof: the clickable prototype — the Schedules list, the create flow with live email preview, the approval gate, the phone-sized executive email, and a failed delivery. Press ▶ Tour there for the guided version.

1 · Reading between the lines

"Every Monday morning I rebuild the same complaint summary by hand and email it out to our sponsor bank… I just want Zanko to put that exact rundown together and have it ready for me each week." — Compliance Manager, mid-size lender "Dana" below

Between the lines: a fixed report on a fixed cadence to an external, regulated stakeholder. "Ready for me" — not "sent for me." She wants the rebuild automated but the send under her control: an approval gate, not a fire-and-forget blast.

"Different people need different cuts of the same information… I'd love to decide once who gets what, and then stop thinking about it." — Customer Operations Lead, fintech "Leo" below

Between the lines: one source report, several audiences, configured once. He isn't asking for more reports — he's asking to stop being the human mail-merge between one report and its readers.

"Honestly, I don't log into the tool… a clean monthly summary I can actually read on my phone before a board meeting — top trends, biggest risks, are we getting better or worse." — VP of Customer Experience "Priya" below

Between the lines: for executives the email is the product. She'll never be a Zanko user, and that's fine — she needs a phone-readable digest with direction-of-travel ("better or worse"), not a login.

The synthesis: all three are downstream of the same missing capability. Zanko can already make the artifact (the existing "Weekly Complaint Review" is literally "Generated by Ask Zanko") — nobody is served by how it reaches people. Dana redistributes it weekly, Leo redistributes it per-audience, Priya wants it to arrive on its own. One feature closes all three loops.

One assumption, stated: "schedule an existing report" treats a report as a re-runnable definition, not a point-in-time snapshot. If Dana's Monday rundown is ad-hoc today (her "by hand" suggests it might be), the same flow covers her: any generated report gets a "Make this recurring" action, which saves the definition and opens the schedule form. Either way, authoring stays out of scope — v1 only adds the re-run-and-deliver loop.

Who it's for: the configurers are ops/compliance leads (Dana, Leo) — existing Zanko users who own a reporting obligation. The beneficiaries include people who will never log in (Priya, the sponsor bank). A feature here is judged by how little the recipients have to do: nothing.

3 · What's in v1 — and what's deliberately not

In

  • A Schedules tab in Reporting: list of schedules with status-first rows (sent / awaiting approval / failed / paused), owner, cadence, next run. R5 R8 R9
  • A create flow in four decisions: source report → cadence → recipients with a version each → delivery options (subject, review-before-send). A live email preview sits beside it the whole time — you see exactly what each audience gets before you commit. R1 R3 R7
  • Two versions, not N: Full report and Executive summary. Two covers every quote; per-recipient custom filtering is configuration sprawl v1 doesn't need. R3 R4
  • The approval gate: schedules with external recipients default to "review before send" — Monday morning Dana gets one Approve & send button, not an hour of rebuilding. R7
  • Send history per schedule with delivery status — the compliance answer to "did the bank get it, and which version?" R6
  • Pause, don't delete: pausing preserves the history; deletion is possible but the history survives as an audit record. R5 R6

Out (and why)

  • No report authoring or custom builder — Zanko already generates the reports; the unmet need in every quote is distribution. Building an editor here would be solving a problem nobody stated.
  • No Slack/Teams/webhooks — the named audiences (sponsor bank, a VP before a board meeting) live in email. Channels can be v2 once the email loop proves out.
  • No per-recipient custom filters or N-version designer — a fair reading of Leo's "different cuts" could mean filtered slices (my team, my product line), not just detail levels. But his own two audiences ("detailed breakdown" vs. "headline numbers") are detail-level, so two verbosity tiers is the honest v1 reduction; slice-level filters multiply preview, approval, and audit surface and wait for evidence.
  • No multi-report digests — bundling several reports into one email is real but separable; v1 keeps one schedule = one report so history and approval stay legible.
  • No open/read tracking — delivery status yes, surveillance no; "did the VP read it" is a v2 conversation with legal, not a v1 default.

4 · Design decisions

The three decisions that matter most

1 — Distribution, not authoring. The feature schedules reports Zanko already makes. That keeps v1 small, honest to the quotes, and scalable — anything the Reports tab can generate, the Schedules tab can deliver.

2 — Audience versions are a property of the recipient, not the report. One schedule, one source of truth, each recipient tagged Full or Executive — Leo decides once; nobody maintains three diverging reports.

3 — Trust is a feature: approval + history + loud failure. In a regulated context, an automated send to a sponsor bank must be reviewable before it leaves and provable after it does. That's what separates this from a cron job.

Information design

DecisionWhy (traced)
Schedules is a third tab beside Dashboards and Reports, reusing the existing list layout, search, and row idiom of the Reports tab. It reads as the Reporting area growing, not a bolt-on. R8
Status leads every row (sent / awaiting approval / failed / paused), then name, cadence, recipients, next run. The owner's Monday question is "does anything need me?" — answered by scanning one column. R5 R9
The live preview is half the create screen, switchable between Full, Executive, and a phone frame. You configure what people receive; you should be looking at it while you decide. R3 R4
The executive email leads with a verdict line ("Overall: improving — volume up, resolution faster, two themes to watch") above four big deltas. In v1 the verdict is computed from KPI direction — deterministic delta logic, not free-form AI prose — so nothing mailed to a VP or a bank can hallucinate. Priya's literal question is "are we getting better or worse" — so the email answers it in its first line. R4

Behavioral design

DecisionWhy (traced)
External recipients flip "review before send" on by default. The owner can turn it off, but the safe path is the default path. Unapproved sends hold and re-notify — nothing external ever auto-sends on a timeout; the gate is the point. (Yes, this reintroduces a weekly click Leo wanted to eliminate — deliberate friction, only where the audience is regulated.) A sponsor bank send is a compliance artifact; defaults carry the judgment. R7
External sends carry the summary plus an expiring secure link — full complaint data never rides in the email body. Internal recipients (Priya) get the digest in-body, since "read on my phone without logging in" is her whole use case. Emailing regulated data outside the org deserves a colder default than emailing your own VP. R2 R7
Approving is one click from the list — review the rendered email, Approve & send. Editing reopens the schedule, not the report. Dana's hour becomes a minute; friction only where the stakes are. R7 R2
Failures notify the owner and stay red in the list until retried or resolved. A silent failure to a regulator-adjacent audience is the worst outcome this feature can have. R9
Every send writes a history row (version, recipients, timestamp, delivery status), kept even if the schedule is paused or deleted. "Did they get it?" must have a provable answer. R6

Feasibility note. This leans on one thing Zanko demonstrably has — report generation ("Generated by Ask Zanko" on the example report) — plus two new primitives: outbound email rendering and a scheduler with an approval queue. The executive summary derives from the same data as the full report (headline KPIs, deltas, top themes), and its verdict line is deterministic delta logic, so no new analytics or AI outputs are required for v1. Cadence includes day and time (Dana's report must land before Monday 8 AM), and each run covers the period since the last.

How I'd know it worked

Three signals: recurring manual exports drop (assuming export events are logged, which Zanko's data-capture posture implies); schedules survive (created-then-paused-forever means the config didn't fit the need); and approval latency (if Dana approves within minutes every Monday, the gate is working — if approvals pile up, the default needs tuning).
